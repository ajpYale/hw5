What the evidence shows

Sources: output/audit_trail.json (64 steps across 4 runs), output/resolved_tickets.json, the Expected/Actual sections and the Cash tab.

  • Routing was right every time. Each ticket went to the planned first agent (101 → Inventory, 102 → Facilities, 103 → Accounting) and only used the agents it needed. Facilities sat out 101 and 103. Inventory and Customer Service sat out 102.
  • The money was right. Every prepared amount came from the DB: $840 (invoice 501), $2,400 (lease 1), $1,044 (10% override) and $264 (12 × $22). The cash math balances exactly: $3,400.00 − $840.00 − $2,400.00 = $160.00 = cash_accounts.checking.
  • The guardrails held. The run had 0 errors and hit no limits: at most 18 of 30 model requests per run and 3 of 6 delegations. The 103 re-run created no duplicate requests. Request #4 ($264) can't be approved against $160.
  • Coordination was the main cost. 43 of the 69 tool calls (62%) were overhead: get_ticket 16×, list_approval_requests 15× and delegate 12×. The Boss, which has no shop-data tools, used the most tokens in every run: 96,788 of 216,607, or 45%.
  • Most differences from the plan came from timing, not agent mistakes. The human approved requests while agents were still finishing, which changed the final statuses on 101 and 102.

1. How well did the agents perform on each ticket, and why?

Ticket 101: Bulldog tee Strong

  • What went right: Inventory's check_stock showed 0 size S on hand. Before ordering, Inventory asked Accounting about invoice 501. Accounting's check_invoice(501) found an unpaid, 3-days-overdue $840 "Rush reprint CC-TEE-WHITE S" from Bulldog Print Co. So Inventory did not order a duplicate, and Accounting prepared request #1 ($840), which pays for the reprint already on the way. Customer Service offered M (5), L (3) or XL (2) and never mentioned the invoice or vendor.
  • Why: the Inventory prompt's rule to check whether a restock is already underway, plus Accounting being the only agent allowed to read invoices.
  • Weak spot: the Boss's final report says "payment itself is not confirmed" even though payment #1 existed. The Boss has no check_invoice or list_payments tool, so it couldn't check. Giving the Boss read-only list_payments would fix that.
  • Cost: 58,137 tokens and 17 model requests for a one-tee order.

Ticket 102: Rent due Correct and safe Expensive

  • What went right: Facilities checked the email against lease 1 with verify_rent_due(1) before any money moved: Elm City Properties, $2,400, due 2026-09-02, 2 days after the desk date. Accounting prepared request #2 and flagged the predicted risk: "approving all pending checking requests would leave $160.00."
  • Healthy skepticism: the rent was approved (02:04:23) while Accounting was still writing its report, so the Boss's pending-only check came back empty. Instead of trusting the "pending" claim, the Boss sent Accounting back to reconcile. That's the "don't accept unbacked claims" rule working.
  • It found a real bug in this app: the Boss flagged that decided_at is wall-clock time (2026-10-14) while paid_at is the business date (2026-08-31). The approve route should stamp desk.date_today on both.
  • Cost: 60,078 tokens and 18 requests, the most of any ticket, for the simplest one. The Accounting step that wrote the final report took 46 seconds.

Ticket 103: Bulk hoodie discount Fast and accurate

  • What went right: the Boss sent Accounting and Inventory out in the same step, so the main run took 34 seconds (02:03:35–02:04:09). Accounting compared quote_bulk_discount at 10% and 15% and prepared request #3: 10% off, $52.20/unit, $1,044.00, 57.9% margin. That's a sensible rate for a student club and well inside the 25% cap and 30% margin floor. Inventory's check_stock found 8 of 20, and it prepared request #4 from Bulldog Print Co (12 × $22 = $264, about 2026-09-05), including the cash warning in its report. Customer Service labeled both the discount and the date as pending or estimated.
  • Waste: the "Run again" re-run spent 45,756 tokens re-confirming nothing. Every agent found #3 approved and #4 pending, and the duplicate guards prevented any new request. The board offering Run again on a resolved ticket invites this.

Part of why this went well happened before this run: in the Problem 5 test runs, Accounting once proposed the maximum 25% discount, and the Boss once told Accounting not to prepare a needed payment. Both were fixed in the prompts. Those test runs aren't in this audit trail.

3. What would have been simpler as a single agent with tools?

  • Ticket 102, the clearest case. The job is a straight line: verify_rent_due → list_payments → prepare_payment → update_ticket_status. One agent needs about 4 calls. The team made 19: verify_rent_due 2×, list_payments 3×, list_approval_requests 5×, get_ticket 4×, delegate 3×. Only 7 of the 19 did shop work.
  • Ticket 101's nested hop. Inventory delegated to Accounting only to read one invoice (check_invoice(501)). An agent holding both check_stock and check_invoice would see the reprint in one step.
  • Everyone re-reads the ticket. Each delegated agent's first move was get_ticket, 4× per run, and the Boss spent about 45% of all tokens restating facts between agents.
  • What the team actually bought: tool permissions as a boundary (only Accounting can call prepare_payment/prepare_price_override, and Facilities and Customer Service have no write tools), a privacy wall (Customer Service never saw invoice 501's details), and parallelism (103 in 34 seconds).

Takeaway for this app: most of the safety came from the MCP server and the approve route (amounts from the DB, $2,500 cap, 25%/30% limits, duplicate checks, cash checks, a human approver), and those would protect a single agent just as well. A single "desk agent" would be simpler and cheaper for 101 and 102. The split earns its cost when tasks can run in parallel (103) or when an agent must not see something (Customer Service).

4. Three new problems this team CAN solve with today's tools

A crest mug order

CC-MUG-CREST has 0 on hand (Aisle D). Boss → Inventory: check_stock confirms the stockout, list_vendors("mugs") finds Elm City Gifts (3-day lead), and prepare_purchase_order prices it at $3.50/unit (12 mugs = $42). Boss → Customer Service drafts an "estimated in about 3 days" reply. With $160 in checking and the $264 PO pending, the PO comes back with a cash_warning, so the owner decides which to approve.

A bulk order of 30 Yale Caps

CC-HAT-BLUE: 40 on hand (size OS), $6 cost, $22 list. Boss → Accounting: quote_bulk_discount("CC-HAT-BLUE", "OS", 30, 10) gives $19.80/unit, $594.00, a 69.7% margin, then prepare_price_override. Boss → Inventory: check_stock shows there's no shortfall, so no PO is needed. It's approved with no cash moving, like request #3.

A spoofed or early rent email

An email claims rent is $2,600, payable to a "new account". Boss → Facilities: verify_rent_due(1) shows $2,400 to Elm City Properties, so it's blocked as possible fraud. Even if it got past Facilities, prepare_payment takes the amount from the lease, not the email. A real October notice (due 2026-10-02) would be refused at prepare time, because $160 can't cover $2,400, and Accounting would escalate.

5. Three new problems this team CANNOT solve, and what it would take

Money coming in

No tool records a sale, deposit or customer payment, so cash can only go down. The Yale AI Club's $1,044 order can't be collected, which leaves the $264 PO stuck. Needed: a human-approved record_receipt/record_sale tool that writes a receipts table and raises cash_accounts, plus a Billing agent (or Accounting extended) to invoice customers and match payments to tickets.

Actually fulfilling orders

Nothing can reserve, receive or ship stock. The 8 hoodies on hand and Tauhid's replacement size can't ship, the 501 reprint can't be checked in when it arrives, and QuickShip CT (vendor 3, local courier, 1-day lead) is in the DB but no tool uses it. Needed: a Fulfillment agent with reserve_stock, receive_shipment (increments inventory.qty), book_courier and ship_order, with the unpaid-invoice check built into the tool.

Talking to anyone outside

Customer replies are drafts only. New emails can't become tickets. Nobody can ask Bulldog Print Co the question Inventory raised on 101: invoice 501 states no quantity or arrival date. Needed: a send_message tool gated by human approval like payments, a create_ticket Intake agent for the inbox, and a vendor_order_status tool for a Purchasing agent.
